**A 25-year-old SGI workstation gives up its secrets**

About 10 years ago I bought a second-hand **SGI Octane**, the kind of workstation high-end science and 3D graphics ran on around 2000. It never booted properly, and with no monitor, keyboard or passwords I couldn't get in, so it sat on a shelf.

This week I tried again with Claude, driving it entirely over an old-school serial cable from my MacBook. Along the way we pulled a physically damaged memory stick. We also wrote small stand-in network servers so the Octane could boot SGI's own recovery system over the network, because modern macOS and a 1998 boot ROM don't quite speak the same language. That got us a root shell and both disks.

It turns out the machine was **"donder", a research workstation at Bayer**, probably at the Berkeley, California site. It had **Molecular Simulations Inc. (MSI)** software installed: **Insight II** for protein and molecular modeling, **Cerius²** for materials and polymer simulation, and **Felix** for NMR data processing. Its logs show it was still being backed up every week in 2003–04, before it left Bayer and eventually ended up with me.
